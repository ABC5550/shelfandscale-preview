01 / The catalogue gap

Six hundred lines nobody has listed.

The numbers come from a catalogue audit we ran; the brand stays unnamed.

One British cosmetics brand sells 650 lines through the trade. Fourteen of them are on Amazon.

The other six hundred are not absent because nobody wants them — for many, shoppers search every month and buy something else. Nor should they all go up blindly: the first job is to rank the range by real search demand, and launch in that order.

What is absent is the work: a title written from what people actually type, images that answer the objection, a flat-file that validates. Multiplied by six hundred.

That is a volume problem. Volume problems are solved by a production line, not by another strategy deck.

03 · Human sign-off

One wrong phrase can take a listing down

A medical claim. "Kills 99.9%" without certification. An invented "free-from". Amazon removes listings for a single phrase — so we check every line as we write, and a person signs off each batch. We learned this the hard way, on our own listing. Your account never has to.

07 / Fair questions

The things you would ask on a call — answered here instead.

Couldn't we just use ChatGPT for this?+
For the words — yes, more or less. That is the part everyone can see, and it is the part that has become cheap. A brand with a large catalogue does not hit a wall on words; it hits the wall underneath — the operations that turn a spreadsheet into live pages on your own account without breaking anything:
  • Flat-files with valid productType schemas, validated before upload
  • Parent/child variations that do not trip the abuse filters
  • Five to seven images per SKU to Amazon's spec — at catalogue volume
  • Errors 5665 and 8541, category gating, Brand Registry
  • Policy-safe copy: no medical claims, no invented free-from claims
  • Keyword data — a year of real UK Amazon searches, 1.46M queries, so titles come from recorded demand
Multiply every line by the number of SKUs in your range, and you have the reason those products are still not listed.
How long does a 500-SKU catalogue actually take?+
Your first 50–100 SKUs are live within 10 working days of clean data and sign-off — and the clock starts when your data template and assets are approved, not when the contract is signed. The rest follows in agreed waves of 50–100, with the schedule fixed after the first wave, on what the data and the category actually required. Early waves are producing metrics while later ones are still in production — and those metrics feed back into the build.
Do you need access to our Seller Central?+
Not for the free sample — that is built entirely from public data and your spec sheets. For filing, either a restricted user on your account or you upload our finished files yourself; both work, and nothing goes live without your sign-off either way.
Seller Central, Vendor Central, or hybrid?+
Seller Central first — that is where our production line, keyword base and own accounts run. For Vendor and hybrid brands the content, images and data files are the same; only the upload path differs, and we scope that per case.
Only Amazon UK, or Europe too?+
Amazon UK first — our keyword base and live accounts are British, and that is where demand can be measured properly. A catalogue built once is mostly a translation-and-transformation away from the EU marketplaces; we roll those out as a follow-on once the UK range performs.
Who owns the content, the files and the data?+
You do — outright. Copy, images, A+ layouts, flat-files and the keyword mapping for your range are delivered as files you keep. If we part ways, everything stays with you and keeps working.
What do you need from us to start?+
Your catalogue in whatever form it exists — spreadsheet, trade price list, wholesale portal — then a filled-in copy of our standard CSV template, product photography or samples for imaging, and a brand authorisation letter if we are fixing existing brand pages (Amazon requires it).
How do revisions work, and what is not included?+
One full round of revisions is included in every package; further rounds are billed at an hourly rate published in the quote. Not included in the per-SKU prices: photography of physical samples (quoted separately), Brand Registry and storefront recovery (scoped per case), advertising (part of the retainer), and VAT.
We have fewer than 100 SKUs — is that too small?+
Not too small, just quoted individually rather than at the list rates — the production line pays for itself at volume, and the published prices reflect that. Send the catalogue anyway; the reply costs you nothing.
Who is this not for?+
Single-product launches and private-label testing — the production line is built for range, and at that size a freelancer will serve you better. And we deliberately stay out of pricing, inventory, fulfilment and customer service: your team keeps the commercial controls, we run the shelf.
