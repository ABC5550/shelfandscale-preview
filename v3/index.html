01 / Which one is you

Four ways a catalogue goes missing on Amazon.

Every brand we audited this year was in one of these. The first move and the first price depend on which.

A · Not on Amazon yet

"We sell through the trade and our own site. Amazon is the gap."

A Kent chocolatier, 100+ lines. The only thing under its name on Amazon UK: five reseller pages from 2020, rated 3.7. "Hot chocolate" is a top-5,000 search all year.

First move
Brand Registry, then the 25–50 lines with proven search demand, filed and live in 10 working days.
Start with
Pilot Wave S £2,400 or M £3,900, fixed.
B · Tried once, pages went dead

"We listed years ago. The pages are still there. Nothing sells."

A Yorkshire confectioner, since 1840. 117 pages on Amazon UK, none with a live offer, so Amazon hides them from search. "Fudge gift set" is a top-3,700 search all year.

First move
Inventory every dead ASIN: relist, merge or retire. Rebuild 50 in retail formats, A+ on the top 3, stock planned for FBA. We call it Catalogue Revival.
Start with
Pilot Wave M £3,900, fixed, 10 working days.
C · On Amazon, sold by someone else

"Our brand is there. Resellers or Vendor set the price, the content and the stock."

A gift-food group on Vendor. A store with eight categories and one item in stock. A bath-bomb maker with Brand Registry. One photo per page; a reseller with 34,000 reviews does the selling.

First move
Brand Registry, full content on the 50 lines that carry the range, then the seller question: your own account, hybrid, or resellers under rules. Leaving Vendor is a six-week job we call Vendor Exit.
Start with
Pilot Wave M £3,900 or L £7,400; Vendor Exit adds £1,500 migration setup.
D · Live, and underperforming

"We sell there ourselves. The catalogue is a tenth of the range, or written in trade language."

A British cosmetics brand. 650 lines through the trade, 14 on Amazon. The other 636 are not absent because nobody wants them: shoppers search every month and buy something else.

First move
Rank the range by real search demand and launch in that order. Titles from what people type, seven images to spec, A+ on the top 3.
Start with
Pilot Wave M £3,900 or L £7,400, then waves of 100 at the per-SKU rate.

Several brands in one group, and not sure which goes first? That is a question we answer in the sample. Single product, private-label test? Not us; a freelancer will serve you better.

Whichever door it is, the shape is the same: a range with demand behind it, and pages that do not exist, do not sell, or belong to someone else.

What is missing is the work: a title written from what people actually type, images that answer the objection, a flat-file that validates. Multiplied by the size of the range.

That is a volume problem. Volume problems are solved by a production line, not by another strategy deck. The first fifty go live in ten working days; the rest follow in the order shoppers ask for them.

06 / Fair questions

The things you would ask on a call, answered here instead.

Do you need access to our Seller Central?+
Not for the sample; that is built from public data and your spec sheets. For filing, either a restricted user on your account or you upload our finished files yourself. Both work, and nothing goes live without your sign-off either way.
How long does a 500-SKU catalogue actually take?+
Your first 50–100 SKUs are live within 10 working days of clean data and sign-off; the clock starts when your data template and assets are approved, not when the contract is signed. The rest follows in waves of 50–100, with the schedule fixed after the first wave on what the data and the category actually required. Early waves are producing live numbers while later ones are still in production, and those numbers feed back into the build.
What do you need from us to start?+
Your catalogue in whatever form it exists: spreadsheet, trade price list, wholesale portal. Then a filled-in copy of our standard CSV template, product photography or samples for imaging, and a brand authorisation letter if we are fixing existing brand pages, because Amazon requires it.
Who owns the content, the files and the data?+
You do, outright. Copy, images, A+ layouts, flat-files and the keyword mapping for your range are delivered as files you keep. If we part ways, everything stays with you and keeps working.
How do revisions work, and what is not included?+
One full round of revisions is included in every wave; further rounds are billed at an hourly rate published in the quote. Not included: photography of physical samples (quoted separately), Brand Registry and storefront recovery (scoped per case), advertising spend, and VAT.
Does a person read every listing, or is it automated?+
Both, and in that order. Every line passes automated checks for prohibited claims, style-guide rules and product-type schema before anyone sees it. Then a person reads and signs off each batch before it is filed. We do not claim Amazon never rejects a listing; we claim that when it does, we handle the case, and it has never been for a phrase we wrote.
Couldn't we just use ChatGPT for this?+
For the words, yes, more or less. That is the part everyone can see, and it is the part that has become cheap. A large catalogue does not hit a wall on words; it hits the wall underneath: the operations that turn a spreadsheet into live pages on your own account without breaking anything.
  • Flat-files with valid productType schemas, validated before upload
  • Parent/child variations that do not trip the abuse filters
  • Seven images per SKU to Amazon's spec, at catalogue volume
  • Errors 5665 and 8541, category gating, Brand Registry
  • Policy-safe copy: no medical claims, no invented free-from claims
  • Titles from a year of recorded UK searches with volumes, not from a model's guess
Multiply every line by the number of SKUs in your range, and you have the reason those products are still not listed.
Seller Central, Vendor Central, or hybrid?+
Seller Central first; that is where our production line, keyword base and own accounts run. For Vendor and hybrid brands the content, images and data files are the same, only the upload path differs. If Amazon is winding your Vendor agreement down, Vendor Exit is the package for it: Seller account, Brand Registry, catalogue migrated in waves, resellers brought under rules.
We own several brands. Which goes first?+
We run all of them through the same demand analysis and tell you which five to ten to back, who earns on them today instead of you, and which to leave alone. Optimising everything does not pay at £3–5 a unit. Send the list; the sample covers up to five brands.
Only Amazon UK, or Europe too?+
Amazon UK first; our keyword base and live accounts are British, and that is where demand can be measured properly. A catalogue built once is mostly a translation-and-transformation away from the EU marketplaces; we roll those out as a follow-on once the UK range performs.
We have fewer than 100 SKUs. Too small?+
Not too small; Pilot Wave S exists for exactly that. Below 25 lines the production line stops paying for itself, and we say so in the reply.
Who is this not for?+
Single-product launches and private-label testing: the production line is built for range, and at that size a freelancer will serve you better. And we stay out of pricing, inventory, fulfilment and customer service. Your team keeps the commercial controls; we run the shelf.
