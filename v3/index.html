01 / Sound familiar

Four ways a catalogue goes missing on Amazon.

Every brand we audited this year was in one of these. The numbers are theirs; the names are not ours to give.

A · Not on Amazon yet

"We sell through the trade and our own site. Amazon is the gap."

A Kent chocolatier with 100+ lines. The only thing under its name on Amazon UK: five reseller pages from 2020, rated 3.7.

B · Tried once, pages went dead

"We listed years ago. The pages are still there. Nothing sells."

A Yorkshire confectioner since 1840. 117 pages on Amazon UK, none with a live offer, so Amazon hides them from search.

C · Sold by someone else

"Our brand is there. Resellers or Vendor set the price, the content and the stock."

A gift-food group on Vendor: a store with eight categories and one item in stock. A bath-bomb maker: one photo per page, a reseller doing the selling.

D · Live, and underperforming

"We sell there ourselves. The catalogue is a tenth of the range."

A British cosmetics brand: 650 lines through the trade, 14 on Amazon. The rest are searched every month; shoppers buy something else.

Whichever it is, the shape is the same: a range with demand behind it, and pages that do not exist, do not sell, or belong to someone else.

What is missing is the work: a title written from what people actually type, images that answer the objection, a flat-file that validates. Multiplied by the size of the range.

That is a volume problem, and volume problems are solved by a production line. We rank the range by real search demand, put the first fifty live in ten working days, and follow in the order shoppers ask for.

06 / Fair questions

The things you would ask on a call, answered here instead.

Do you need access to our Seller Central?+
Not for the sample; that is built from public data and your spec sheets. For filing, either a restricted user on your account or you upload our finished files yourself. Both work, and nothing goes live without your sign-off either way.
How long does a 500-SKU catalogue actually take?+
Your first 50–100 SKUs are live within 10 working days of clean data and sign-off; the clock starts when your data template and assets are approved, not when the contract is signed. The rest follows in waves of 50–100, with the schedule fixed after the first wave on what the data and the category actually required.
What do you need from us to start?+
Your catalogue in whatever form it exists. Then a filled-in copy of our standard CSV template, product photography or samples for imaging, and a brand authorisation letter if we are fixing existing brand pages, because Amazon requires it.
Who owns the content, the files and the data?+
You do, outright. Copy, images, A+ layouts, flat-files and the keyword mapping for your range are delivered as files you keep. If we part ways, everything stays with you and keeps working.
How do revisions work, and what is not included?+
One full round of revisions is included in every wave; further rounds are billed at an hourly rate published in the quote. Not included: photography of physical samples, Brand Registry and storefront recovery, advertising spend, and VAT.
Does a person read every listing, or is it automated?+
Both, in that order. Every line passes automated checks for prohibited claims, style-guide rules and product-type schema. Then a person reads and signs off each batch before it is filed. We do not claim Amazon never rejects a listing; we claim that when it does, we handle the case.
Couldn't we just use ChatGPT for this?+
For the words, more or less. A large catalogue does not hit a wall on words; it hits the wall underneath: the operations that turn a spreadsheet into live pages on your own account without breaking anything.
  • Flat-files with valid productType schemas, validated before upload
  • Parent/child variations that do not trip the abuse filters
  • Seven images per SKU to Amazon's spec, at catalogue volume
  • Errors 5665 and 8541, category gating, Brand Registry
  • Policy-safe copy: no medical claims, no invented free-from claims
  • Titles from a year of recorded UK searches, not from a model's guess
Seller Central, Vendor Central, or hybrid?+
Seller Central first; that is where our production line, keyword base and own accounts run. For Vendor and hybrid brands the content, images and data files are the same, only the upload path differs. If Amazon is winding your Vendor agreement down, we set up the Seller account, Brand Registry and the migration in waves.
We own several brands. Which goes first?+
We run all of them through the same demand analysis and tell you which five to ten to back, who earns on them today instead of you, and which to leave alone. The sample covers up to five brands.
Only Amazon UK, or Europe too?+
Amazon UK first; our keyword base and live accounts are British, and that is where demand can be measured properly. A catalogue built once is mostly a translation away from the EU marketplaces; we roll those out once the UK range performs.
We have fewer than 100 SKUs. Too small?+
Pilot Wave S exists for exactly that. Below 25 lines the production line stops paying for itself, and we say so in the reply.
Who is this not for?+
Single-product launches and private-label testing: at that size a freelancer will serve you better. And we stay out of pricing, inventory, fulfilment and customer service. Your team keeps the commercial controls; we run the shelf.
