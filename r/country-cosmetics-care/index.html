WHAT THE PAGES SAY

Your best sellers say almost nothing about what is in the bottle.

Your Witch Hazel 300ml is your best seller: Amazon's Choice, 300+ bought in past month, and the 4th result when someone searches "witch hazel". It has two photos, no A+, and About this item is four lines: the name, "Product type: ASTRINGENT SUBSTANCE", the brand and the size. Your 500ml and your Vitamin E Oil 300ml read the same way.

Your bottle says what the page doesn't: alcohol free, a clear, colourless distillate, gentler than witch hazel with alcohol. Shoppers search "witch hazel alcohol free"; none of your titles on sale says it.

So: five real lines and seven photos on each best seller, best sellers first: five rebuilds and new photos for four pages a month on Care S.

WHAT A SHOPPER SEES

Small things a shopper trips over.

On your Lavender Water 1 litre, Amazon names the scent "Floral", so the 300ml and 500ml sit behind a second button. On your Rose Water 500ml and 1 litre the scent is "Floral" too, with the 300ml under "Rose".

Your Witch Hazel 1 litre lists "Natural first aid" and "Anti-inflammatory and antiseptic"; your Rose Water 500ml and 1 litre say "Recommended for acne and skin irritation". On a skin care page, lines like these are a risk to put right, not a fault.

So: every size reachable from every page, and the medical-sounding lines written as skin care.
