A RISK, NOT AN ACCUSATION

Two claims a food page cannot make.

Your chia seeds title says "for Weight Loss" and ends "Weightloss". Four psyllium husk titles say "Laxatives Constipation Relief". In the UK a food may not claim to treat a condition, and may carry only the health claims on the authorised register; we find no weight-loss claim there for chia seeds. Amazon can remove pages over claims like these.

"Chia seeds for weight loss" is searched (7,231st of 1.46 million phrases), so the words were not chosen at random. The page can be found as chia seeds without making the claim.

So: the weight-loss and laxative claims taken off the titles and the bullets.

WHAT A SHOPPER SEES

Three main photos show a 2020 or 2022 best-before date.

The first photo is the one a shopper sees on the search page. Of the sixteen main photos we zoomed into, three show a batch label with a best-before date: the only photo on your best-selling Brazil nut page shows a best-before date of May 2022 and a warehouse barcode sticker; the goji berries photo shows February 2022 and the same kind of sticker; the almonds photo shows February 2020. It is a fact about the photos, not about the stock in the warehouse.

So: those photos replaced with a clean pack shot like the one in the example, inside the monthly photo quota.

WHAT IS ALREADY RIGHT

The account side is in order.

You hold the featured offer on all 94 pages that can be bought, 76 of them dispatched by Amazon. Every one of the 100 links to the PURIMA store, and 75 carry the Amazon's Choice badge.

So the work is on the pages themselves: photos, A+, titles that say what the product is, and the six pages that show "Currently unavailable".

So: no change of seller, account or price; the pages brought up to the standard of your best ones.
