WHAT A SHOPPER SEES

Two wrong unit prices; four shades, one jar.

Under the price, Amazon works out what a kilo or 100 ml costs from the size you entered. Your Natural Insect Repellent Balm, a 35 g jar at £14.99, shows £14,990.00 a kilo: the size behind it reads one gram. The Lavender and Calamine Night Gel, a 50 ml bottle at £10.05, shows £10.05 per 100 ml; the true figure is £20.10.

Veil Cover Cream in Latte, Natural, Almond and Tan, four shades at £25.99, use one and the same main photo: a closed jar with a white lid. Someone choosing a shade can't see it. Your Walnut, Mocha and No 2 pages show the cream itself, so the photos exist.

So: the size set right on every page, so the price per unit reads true, and each shade shown in its own colour; the weekly check catches the next slip.

WHAT THE PAGES SAY

21 of the 40 pages carry one photo, and none has A+.

20 of them are Veil, every Veil page we read: the 10 g shades, the 19 g, 44 g and 70 g pots at up to £51.99, the finishing powder and the concealer sets. A cover cream is bought on its shade and its finish, and on these pages there is one picture to judge both.

Your ten best sellers, the calamine creams and lotions among them, have one to six photos; none of the 40 pages has A+ or a video.

So: seven photos on each page, best sellers first: five rebuilds and new photos for four pages a month on Care S.

WHAT THE NAMES SAY

Eight names on the packs, one brand name on Amazon.

All 40 pages say Veil Cover Cream as the brand: the Calm & Calamine creams and lotions, Pernioderm, The Witch Hazel Experts, the British Experts yellow soft paraffin, CeraFresh, the Fe2 Minerals gels and lotions, and the insect balm, whose jar reads Ebenezer Magic Balm. Two Calm & Calamine titles open with "Veil Cover Cream". Your Traditional Calamine Cream says "over 50 years"; the All Purpose cream says "over 70 years". The 50 ml All Purpose cream sits on a page of its own, outside the 100 ml and 200 ml picker.

Some lines say more than a skin cream may: "great for joint pain", "fight off infections", "Phenol is toxic to the body … unlike some traditional brands". Lines like these can get a page held for review.

So: each page named as its pack is, one story of the company on every page, and lines that read as treatment claims rewritten before Amazon asks.
