WHAT A SHOPPER SEES

Three of your sets show a unit price of £50,000 a litre or more.

The Body Building shampoo and conditioner duo (B0BXPTDXYR, "200+ bought in past month") shows £50.00 and, beside it, "(£50,000.00 / l)"; the size selected reads "0.5 ml (Pack of 2)". The Flaky/Itchy set (B0BXPVDDRF, "100+") shows £55,000.00 / l, the Moisture Balancing set (B0BXPWTM1B, "50+") £50,000.00 / l, and the Body Building collection of four (B0B14PSWYY, "100+") £3,000.00 per 100 ml.

Smaller things a shopper can read: three pages spell it "Anit-Dandruff" and one "Silican", and the Swimcap's title says "Heat Damage" where its bullets say sun and chlorine.

So: the size and unit fields corrected so the price per litre reads true, the spelling fixed, and the title and bullets made to agree.

WHAT A SHOPPER CANNOT BUY

58 of the 100 pages we read have no featured offer today.

On 34 of them Amazon says "Currently unavailable. We don't know when or if this item will be back in stock."; 11 of the 34 carry A+ content, so the pages are built. The other 24 show no buy box and no such message: the 40 ml Elasticizer (B072HNMQQR), for one, shows "See All Buying Options" and "1 option from £13.00" in its size selector. All 28 pages that show a sales label can be bought.

Why a page is dark, or has no featured offer, is not visible from outside, and some may be meant to be: stock, range and price are yours to set.

So: the weekly check lists every such page, asks you which are meant to be dark, and files the fix that is ours to file: cases, attributes, pages back on sale.

A RISK, NOT AN ACCUSATION

One bullet sits close to the line between a cosmetic and a medicine.

The Density Thickening Shampoo (B0CTYGGWT5, "100+ bought in past month") says it is "designed to help prevent hair loss", and its third bullet talks of "hair loss prevention" products. Hair loss is a medical condition, and a cosmetic that is presented as preventing it is close to the cosmetic and medicine line.

We make no finding and this is not a legal opinion: whether the wording is right is for your regulatory team to judge. Amazon can ask for a page to be looked at over words like these.

So: the weekly check lists lines like this for that team, with plainer wording proposed where you ask. Nothing changes without your approval, and a claim never goes into search terms.
